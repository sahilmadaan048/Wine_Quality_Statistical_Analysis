# Part A — One-Sample t-test

### 🎤 SCRIPT

> “First, we performed a **one-sample t-test** on alcohol.
>
> We wanted to test whether the mean alcohol content differs from 10%.”

Put the hypotheses verbally:

> “Our null hypothesis was:
>
> **H₀: μ = 10**
>
> and our alternative hypothesis was:
>
> **H₁: μ ≠ 10.**”

Then explain why the test:

> “We used a one-sample t-test because we were comparing the mean of one sample with a specified reference value, while the population standard deviation was unknown.”

### Results

> “The calculated t-statistic was approximately **14.73**, and the p-value was approximately **1.16 × 10⁻⁴⁵**.”

Pause.

> “Since this p-value is far below our significance level of 0.05, we **reject the null hypothesis**.”

### Meaning

> “So statistically, we have very strong evidence that the mean alcohol content in the population represented by our data is different from 10%.”

# Most important result

Point to graph/table.

> “The key result was that as sample size increased, the estimates became more stable.”

Give the actual evidence:

> “The standard deviation of the sample means decreased from approximately **0.163 at n = 50** to approximately **0.016 at n = 1000**.”

> “Similarly, the average confidence interval width decreased from approximately **0.621** to **0.134**.”

## Explain WHY

This is the part you absolutely need to explain.

> “This happens because the standard error of the mean is approximately:
>
> \[
> SE = \frac{s}{\sqrt n}
> \]
>
> So when the sample size increases, the denominator √n increases, causing the standard error to decrease.”

Then:

> “A smaller standard error means our estimate of the mean becomes more precise, which produces narrower confidence intervals.”

---

# Explain sample means

### What does this prove?

Be careful.

Don't say:

❌ “This proves 10.4323 is the true population mean.”

Instead:

> “This demonstrates that random sampling creates variation between individual samples, but as sample size increases, the sample estimates become increasingly stable around the dataset's reference mean.”
