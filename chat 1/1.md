# 15. 95% confidence interval

We calculated:

\[
[10.3747,\;10.4899]

This is our 95% confidence interval for mean alcohol.

The formula is:

\[
\bar{x}\pm t_{\alpha/2,n-1}\frac{s}{\sqrt n}

You don't necessarily need to derive this on the board.

Understand the components:

- \(\bar{x}\) = sample mean
- \(s\) = sample standard deviation
- \(n\) = sample size
- \(t\) = critical value

For your presentation, a simpler version is:

> **"The interval gives a range of plausible values for the population mean based on our sample and the 95% confidence procedure."**

# 42. Why 5-fold cross-validation?

We don't want to train and evaluate on exactly the same data.

Otherwise the model could look artificially good.

So we use:

\[
5\text{-fold cross-validation}
\]

Conceptually:

```text
Fold 1 → validation
Fold 2 → validation
Fold 3 → validation
Fold 4 → validation
Fold 5 → validation
```

Each time:

4 folds train the model.

1 fold evaluates it.

Eventually every observation gets used as validation data once.

---

# 43. Metrics

We used:

### MAE

Mean Absolute Error.

Average absolute prediction error.

Lower is better.

---

### MSE

Mean Squared Error.

Large errors are penalized more strongly.

Lower is better.

---

### RMSE

Square root of MSE.

\[
RMSE=\sqrt{MSE}
\]

Lower is better.

It's particularly easy to explain because it is expressed in the same units as quality.

---

### R²

Measures how much variation is explained relative to a mean-based baseline.

Higher is better.

Important:

> **R² is not accuracy.**
