# PART 13 — Why did we use the t-distribution?

Because we are estimating a population mean using a sample standard deviation.

Formula:

\[
\bar{x}\pm t_{\alpha/2,n-1}\frac{s}{\sqrt n}
\]

We have:

```text
degrees of freedom = n - 1
                   = 1358
```

For large n, the t-distribution becomes very close to the normal distribution.

That's why:

```text
t-critical ≈ 1.9617
```

which is very close to:

```text
1.96
```

### Q89. Why did you use Welch's test instead of ordinary independent t-test?

> Because Welch's test does not assume equal population variances, making it more appropriate when the two groups may have different variances.

### Q90. Why not use a classification model?

> Because our project treats quality as a numerical score and aims to predict the score itself. Therefore we used regression models. If we had converted quality into categories such as low/high and treated those as classes, classification metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall and F1 could have been used instead.

### Q91. Why not say your model has 38.53% accuracy?

> Because 0.3853 is R², not accuracy. R² measures explained variation, whereas accuracy is generally a classification metric.

### Q92. Why did Random Forest beat Linear Regression?

> Random Forest can capture nonlinear relationships and interactions between physicochemical variables, whereas Linear Regression models an additive linear relationship. The better performance suggests that nonlinearities or interactions may be useful, although the improvement is moderate.

---

### Q93. Why isn't Random Forest R² very high?

> The physicochemical variables do not fully explain the sensory quality score. Quality can be subjective, other relevant variables may be absent, and the relationship among variables can be complex.

---

### Q94. Why does alcohol have the highest feature importance?

> In this Random Forest model, alcohol contributes the most to the model's predictive splits according to the feature-importance measure. This is consistent with alcohol also having the strongest positive correlation with quality among the predictors. However, this does not imply causation.

---

### Q95. Why does the p-value become smaller with larger samples?

> When a genuine difference exists, increasing sample size decreases the standard error, which increases the magnitude of the test statistic and therefore can produce stronger evidence against the null hypothesis.

---

### Q96. Why are confidence intervals narrower with larger samples?

> Because the standard error is proportional to \(1/\sqrt n\). Increasing n reduces standard error and therefore reduces the margin of error.

---

### Q97. Why isn't CI coverage exactly 95%?

> Because empirical coverage is based on only 30 repetitions at each sample size. With only 30 repetitions, 28/30 gives 93.33% and 29/30 gives 96.67%, so fluctuations around 95% are expected.

---

### Q98. Does significant correlation prove alcohol causes quality?

> No. This is observational data, so correlation only indicates association. Other variables and processes could influence both alcohol and quality.
